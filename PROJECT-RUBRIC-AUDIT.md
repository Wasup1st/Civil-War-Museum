# Honors Museum Exhibit: Rubric Audit

Project: Civil War Medicine: Two Sides of Care
Live exhibit: https://wasup1st.github.io/Civil-War-Museum/

## What is already in the interactive exhibit

- Historical theme and central argument: 1861-1865 medical care, including how Union and Confederate hospital systems handled similar needs with different resources and labor systems.
- Audience: students and general visitors. The site offers first-person exploration, a guided tour, a map, and a 2D reading mode.
- Five major exhibit features: (1) medical context, (2) Union Satterlee General Hospital, (3) Confederate Chimborazo Hospital, (4) interpretive Union field hospital, and (5) later medical influence.
- Fourteen exhibits within those five areas. The assignment calls for three to five **key items or features**. In the proposal, present the five themed galleries as the five key features, then list individual artifacts under them.
- Firsthand evidence: Margaret Hamilton's nursing recollection, Phoebe Yates Pember's 1879 memoir, and Army of the Potomac General Orders No. 147 from 1862. Their direct words are labeled as quotations.
- Other original evidence: 1864 Satterlee lithograph, 1865 Chimborazo image, 1864 Petersburg medical-team photograph, historical hospital photographs, and the George Ivy letter catalog from January 1864.
- Source distinction: John Worrall's experience comes from medical case records, not his own words. George Ivy's full letter text and final medical outcome are not verified, so the exhibit cites the catalog and does not invent either.
- A collection of 14 archival image references and seven links to historical letters, orders, and firsthand records. All image captions identify actual locations and workers, so photographs of other hospitals do not get labeled as Satterlee or Chimborazo.
- Perspectives include physicians, nurses, hospital patients, and enslaved African American workers. Present-day impact is discussed in the final gallery. The exhibit distinguishes wartime organization from later medical discoveries.
- A bibliography includes at least two credible scholarly works, plus a scholarly journal article and archives. The Devine and Humphreys books are background reading identified in the bibliography. Exact chapter and page citations must be checked before quoting specific points from them in the written proposal.

## The separate written proposal is still required

The instructor requests a single Word or PDF document with clearly labeled sections:

1. **Exhibit Overview (0.5-1 page):** title, topic, intended audience, purpose, and why the visitor should care.
2. **Historical Context and Argument (1-2 pages):** time period, clear argument, key historical facts and analysis, multiple perspectives, and at least two credible secondary sources.
3. **Reflection (1-2 pages):** explain your personal choice of topic, the actual research and coding decisions you made, feedback from office hours, what you learned, and what surprised you. Write this from your own experience rather than inventing reflections.
4. **Works Cited:** a consistent citation style for everything referenced.

The interactive website is the separate **Exhibit Design Plan**. Its five major galleries need short explanations in the written proposal explaining how their artifacts support the central argument. The site is not a replacement for the overview, context/argument, reflection, or Works Cited document.

## Final visual tests

- Walk the full route from the introduction to both hospital wings, field hospital, and legacy gallery.
- Look for open gaps or wall overlap where the side corridors meet the North and South galleries.
- Check labels at normal screen size. Confirm none are clipped.
- Open each of the fourteen exhibits and inspect the archival image selector and original-record links.
- Check in a mobile browser and use the 2D reader.
- Check whether images hosted outside GitHub load. If a preview fails, the archival record link remains the authoritative source.

## Evidence and ethical interpretation

The field-hospital room is a representative reconstruction, not an exact surviving building. The staff GLBs are role illustrations, not historical likenesses. The North/South lighting is a design choice, not a value judgment about medical workers or patients. The exhibit gives the role of enslaved labor at Chimborazo clear historical context.

The goal is a historically accurate, readable, and engaging exhibit. Do not treat a good-looking museum as a substitute for source accuracy or the written assignment.
